Q1: What is UCAS?

UCAS stands for the Universities and Colleges Admissions Service. It is the main system used to apply for undergraduate courses at UK universities.

For 2027 entry, one UCAS application allows a student to select up to five university or course choices.

UCAS normally does not award the scholarship. Scholarships are usually provided by:

  • The university itself
  • A university department
  • The UK government
  • A charity or external organisation
  • A student’s national government or sponsor

Q2: Can an Iraqi student use UCAS?

Yes. Iraqi students can apply through UCAS as international applicants.

However, every university decides which Iraqi qualifications it accepts. Having an Iraqi secondary certificate does not guarantee direct entry into the first year of every UK university.

Some universities may require:

  • An International Foundation Year
  • A-level qualifications
  • The International Baccalaureate Diploma
  • Another recognised international qualification
  • Evidence of English-language ability

For example, the University of Exeter currently states that the Iraqi National High School Diploma is not sufficient for direct undergraduate entry at Exeter. A recognised foundation programme may be required. Other universities can have different rules.

Q3: What types of scholarships are available?

  1. Automatic scholarships: the university assesses the student automatically after receiving the course application.
  2. Competitive merit scholarships: based on excellent grades, activities, achievements and the quality of the application.
  3. Need-based scholarships: based partly or entirely on the student’s financial circumstances.
  4. Country or regional scholarships: available only to applicants from certain countries or regions, such as Iraq or the Middle East.
  5. Subject scholarships: available only for particular courses such as engineering, science, business, music or sport.
  6. Tuition-fee discounts: reduce the amount paid to the university but do not provide living expenses.
  7. Full scholarships: may cover all tuition and sometimes living costs. These are uncommon and extremely competitive at undergraduate level.

Important UCAS dates for 2027 entry

Date What happens?
12 May 2026 Students can register and begin their 2027 application.
1 September 2026 Completed undergraduate applications can be submitted.
15 October 2026 Deadline for Oxford, Cambridge and most Medicine, Dentistry and Veterinary courses.
13 January 2027 Equal-consideration deadline for most undergraduate courses.

The 2027 UCAS application fee is £34.50. This permits up to five undergraduate choices.

Scholarship deadlines can be much earlier or later than the UCAS course deadline. Students must check both deadlines separately.

How should an Iraqi student apply?

  1. Decide which subject you want to study.
  2. Search for suitable courses using the official UCAS course search.
  3. Check each university’s entry requirements specifically for Iraqi qualifications.
  4. Check whether a Foundation Year is required.
  5. Check English-language requirements.
  6. Calculate the complete tuition and living costs.
  7. Search both the UCAS scholarship tool and the university’s own scholarship page.
  8. Create the UCAS application and add up to five choices.
  9. Complete the education history and obtain a reference.
  10. Write the three UCAS personal-statement answers honestly and in your own voice.
  11. Submit before the correct course deadline.
  12. After receiving an offer, complete every separate scholarship application.
  13. Keep checking email, including the spam folder.

Documents commonly requested

Requirements vary, but students may need:

  • Passport or valid travel document
  • Official secondary-school certificates
  • Grade transcripts
  • Certified English translations
  • Predicted grades if still studying
  • An academic reference
  • English-language test results when required
  • UCAS personal-statement answers
  • Scholarship essays or additional application forms
  • Evidence of family income for need-based scholarships

The university decides exactly which documents and translations are acceptable.

⚠️ A partial scholarship is not full funding

A scholarship described as “£5,000” may only subtract £5,000 from the tuition fee. It may not cover:

  • The remaining tuition fee
  • Accommodation
  • Food and daily expenses
  • Flights
  • Visa costs
  • Health surcharge
  • Books and equipment

Always calculate the amount still unpaid after the scholarship.

Student visa financial requirement

A scholarship does not automatically remove the UK Student visa financial requirement.

Under the current published rules, a student may need to prove they can pay the unpaid course fees shown on their Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies, plus:

  • £1,529 per month for up to nine months when studying in London.
  • £1,171 per month for up to nine months when studying outside London.

Personal or parental funds normally have to remain in an acceptable account for at least 28 consecutive days. Evidence must meet the current visa rules.

These amounts can change. Always check the official UK Government website before applying for a visa.

What about postgraduate scholarships?

Chevening is available to eligible applicants from Iraq, but it is for eligible one-year taught master’s degrees—not undergraduate study.

Chevening is applied for through its own application system, not through the undergraduate UCAS application. Its current application cycle is closed.

Iraq is not included in the British Council’s published list of countries eligible for GREAT Scholarships in the 2026–27 academic year. Students should check again in future years because the country list can change.

Official Chevening Iraq page →

🚨 Avoid scholarship scams

  • Do not pay anyone who guarantees a scholarship.
  • Check that the address belongs to UCAS, GOV.UK, Chevening or the university itself.
  • Do not send passport copies or bank documents to unknown social-media accounts.
  • Never publish personal documents on a public website.
  • Confirm every scholarship on the university’s official website before applying.
