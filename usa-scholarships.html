Find your route to studying in the USA.

Understand financial aid, compare funding routes, and build a stronger application.

Important scope: There is no honest, permanent list of “every” U.S. scholarship. Thousands of university and private awards change every year, and many are limited to U.S. citizens. This researched guide covers the major nationwide, Iraq-specific and university awards that an Iraqi applicant can realistically pursue, plus official search tools for finding additional awards. Always re-check the linked official page before applying.

Choose your route

Start with your current education level. The application route is different for each group.

🏫 Secondary school Short, funded exchanges such as IYLEP, with current country checks. 🎓 Bachelor's / first-year Need-based aid, full rides, full tuition and automatic merit. 📚 Master's / professional Fulbright, Rotary, Humphrey, P.E.O. and university funding. 🔬 PhD / research Funded admission packages, assistantships and fellowships.

Understand the funding words

Full demonstrated need The college calculates what your family can pay, then covers the rest. It does not automatically mean zero family payment.
Full cost / full ride Usually tuition plus living costs. Flights, insurance, books or personal costs may still be excluded.
Full tuition Pays tuition only. Housing, food, insurance, travel and fees can remain expensive.
Partial scholarship A discount. You must prove that you can fund the unpaid balance before receiving Form I-20.
Funded exchange A short, non-degree programme that may cover travel, housing, meals, visa and programme costs.
Eligible to apply does not mean guaranteed funding. The most generous universities are extremely selective. Build a balanced list that includes several automatic or predictable merit options, and calculate the final amount your family must pay.

Bachelor's funding

Most U.S. colleges give little or no aid to international students. The strongest plan combines two kinds of institution: colleges that meet full demonstrated financial need and universities with large merit scholarships.

Need-blind admission with full demonstrated need

The universities below state that an international first-year applicant's request for aid does not hurt the admission decision and that they meet the institution's calculation of demonstrated need. Transfer rules can be different. Admission remains exceptionally competitive.

Reference international first-year need-based aid policies, checked 12 September 2026
University What the policy can cover What an Iraqi applicant must do
Harvard College Need-blind admission; institutional need-based aid on the same basis regardless of nationality. Aid may include tuition, housing, food, health insurance and other assessed costs. Apply for undergraduate admission and submit Harvard's international financial-aid documents by the stated deadline.
Yale University 100% of demonstrated need, regardless of citizenship; aid awards do not require loans. Submit the CSS Profile and required family income documents. FAFSA is only for eligible U.S. applicants.

Amherst College Need-blind for all applicants and 100% of calculated need met for admitted international students. Indicate that you need aid when applying and submit the international financial-aid materials on time.

Best fit for a student with very high need: apply to several of these colleges, but do not make them your only plan. “Full need” uses the college's own calculation, so compare the family contribution and all non-billable expenses in each offer.

Major merit scholarships open to international applicants

Full billable costs

American University Emerging Global Leader

Coverage: For fall 2027, two awards cover tuition, room and board for four years. They do not cover health insurance, books, flights, taxes and miscellaneous costs—AU estimates about $4,000 per year. Partial awards up to $40,000 per year are also considered.

Requirements: International first-year applicant graduating secondary school by June 2027; strong academics, leadership, service and commitment to improving the home community. Students who already began university are not eligible.

Apply: Common Application, admission documents, AU EGLS application and essays, official English score, bank letter and Declaration of Finances showing at least the uncovered amount. Deadline: 15 January 2027.

Full cost + enrichment

Washington and Lee Johnson Scholarship

Coverage: Tuition, fees, housing and food plus $10,000 for a summer experience; additional need-based aid may be available.

Requirements: Outstanding academic record, leadership, integrity and potential to contribute on campus.

Apply: Common Application plus the Johnson supplement and second essay. Deadline: 1 December 2026. Finalists are invited to a university-paid selection visit.

Full tuition + stipend

Vanderbilt Signature Scholarships

Coverage: Ingram and Cornelius Vanderbilt awards provide full tuition plus summer or enrichment support. International first-year applicants are eligible for Vanderbilt merit scholarships.

Requirements: Exceptional academics plus leadership, service, entrepreneurship or contribution to the chosen field. Recipients are typically among the strongest applicants.

Apply: Submit admission and the separate scholarship application/essay in the applicant portal by 1 December 2026. Use the current official list rather than older scholarship round-ups.

Partial to full merit

Emory University Scholar Programs

Coverage: Awards range from partial merit scholarships to full funding and are renewable for eight semesters.

Requirements: All citizenships are eligible. Selection considers intellectual strength, leadership, creativity and community impact; competition is very high.

Apply: Answer “yes” to scholar consideration on the admission application; no nomination or extra scholar essay is required. Submit admission materials by 15 November 2026 (ED I: 1 November).

Full tuition + living

Richmond Scholars

Coverage: Full tuition, housing and food plus enrichment benefits.

Requirements: International applicants are considered for Richmond merit awards, with one programme-specific exception. Richmond's international admission process is need-aware.

Apply: No separate scholarship application; complete the University of Richmond admission application by 1 December 2026. Submit the international financial certification required for admission.

Half tuition

Boston University Presidential Scholarship

Coverage: Half of tuition, beginning with the fall 2026 entering class.

Requirements: International first-year applicants are eligible; BU looks for exceptional academic achievement, leadership and contribution beyond school.

Apply: No separate scholarship form. Submit the admission application and credentials by 1 December 2026. Budget separately for remaining tuition and all living costs.

Automatic merit

University of Alabama International Freshman Scholarships

Coverage for fall 2027: With at least a 3.50 GPA, official SAT bands award $6,000 (1200–1250), $8,000 (1260–1290), $10,000 (1300–1320), $15,000 (1330–1350), $24,000 (1360–1410) or $28,000 (1420–1600) per year.

Top award: A 4.0+ GPA and 1600 SAT/36 ACT can receive tuition for up to four years, first-year housing, $1,500 annually and a $2,000 research/study allowance.

Apply: Be admitted as a first-time international freshman and submit qualifying official scores by 4 December 2026. Scholarship evaluation does not superscore.

Automatic merit

University of Mississippi International Scholarships

Coverage: Automatic 2026–27 academic awards include $5,000 per year for GPA 3.25–3.49, $9,500 for 3.50–3.74, $12,000 for 3.75–3.89 and $28,893 for 3.90–4.00. The top GPA award is limited and offered in admission order. SAT/ACT awards can reach $20,160 per year; the higher automatic award applies rather than stacking both.

Requirements: Admitted international undergraduate meeting the university's English requirement and qualifying GPA or official score.

Apply: Automatic awards need no separate form. The separate competitive scholarship application is due 10 January; verify the entry-year table before relying on an amount.

Already studying at an Iraqi university? U.S. colleges will usually treat you as a transfer applicant, even if you want to restart. Many full-ride merit awards above are first-year only, and international transfer aid is much rarer. Check the university's transfer definition before paying an application fee.

Master's, PhD and professional opportunities

Graduate funding is often tied to a specific degree or department. Apply to the fellowship and the university programme separately whenever the instructions require both.

Iraq-specific master's

Fulbright Foreign Student Program — Iraq

Coverage: Tuition, living allowance, round-trip travel, J-1 visa sponsorship, health benefits and enrichment activities.

Requirements: Iraqi citizen/permanent resident able to hold an Iraqi passport; reside in Iraq throughout selection; U.S.-equivalent bachelor's degree; strong academics; English ability; and at least two years of professional work after graduation. U.S. citizens/permanent residents are ineligible. Clinical medicine, dentistry, pharmacy and nursing are excluded, but public health and nursing administration may be allowed.

Documents: Transcripts with certified English translations, CV, three English recommendations, personal statement (maximum 600 words), study/research objective, scores if available and passport if available.

Status: The 2026 Iraq application closed after the 1 May deadline. Watch the official page for the next cycle. Awardees must normally return home for at least two years after the programme.

Any Stanford graduate field

Knight-Hennessy Scholars

Coverage: Up to three years of tuition and fees, living/academic stipend, annual economy travel and a one-time relocation stipend. Longer degrees follow the Stanford programme's own funding policy after year three.

Requirements for the 2027 cohort: Applicant from any country; admission to an eligible full-time Stanford graduate programme; first bachelor's degree earned in January 2020 or later (January 2018 for qualifying military service), or completed before enrolment in September 2027.

Apply: Submit both the Knight-Hennessy application by 6 October 2026 at 1:00 p.m. PT and the separate Stanford degree application by its applicable deadline.

Peace master's

Rotary Peace Fellowship

Coverage: Tuition and fees, room and board, round-trip transportation, internship and field-study expenses. U.S. master's study is available through the Duke University–UNC Peace Center.

Requirements: Bachelor's degree, English proficiency, demonstrated peace/development commitment and leadership, at least three years of full-time relevant experience, and generally a three-year gap between the most recent degree and fellowship start. Rotary conflict-of-interest rules apply.

Status: The 2027–28 cycle is closed. Rotary says applications for 2028–29 open in February 2027.

Women · graduate

P.E.O. International Peace Scholarship

Coverage: Need-based grant up to $12,500 for women from outside the U.S. and Canada studying an advanced degree in either country. It is not full funding; applicants must prove other funds.

Requirements: No U.S./Canadian citizenship or permanent residence; at least one full academic year of formal coursework remaining; enrolled and in residence for the full year; commitment to return home and make an impact. Online-only study, dissertation-only first applications and practical training without coursework do not qualify.

Apply: Complete the eligibility form between 15 September and 15 December 2026, then follow the invitation instructions if eligible.

Mid-career · non-degree

Hubert H. Humphrey Fellowship

Programme: Ten months of non-degree graduate-level study and professional development. Iraq is listed among participating countries.

Requirements: Undergraduate degree, at least five years of full-time professional experience, leadership and public-service record, limited prior U.S. experience and English proficiency. Two recommendations—including one from the current employer—and official English transcripts are required.

Apply: Through the U.S. Embassy or the designated nominating office in Iraq; there is no direct university application. Ask for the current Iraq deadline.

English teaching exchange

Fulbright Foreign Language Teaching Assistant

Programme: A non-degree U.S. exchange for early-career English teachers or trainees who teach their home language and take academic courses.

Typical requirements: Bachelor's degree, early-career teaching profile, strong interest in language/cultural exchange and competitive English (Fulbright gives a general recommendation of TOEFL 79–80 or IELTS 6.5). U.S. citizens are ineligible.

Apply: Candidates must be nominated by the U.S. Embassy/Fulbright office; independent applications are not accepted. Confirm whether and when Iraq nominations are open.

For a PhD, search for funded admission—not only a named scholarship. Many U.S. research PhDs support admitted students through a fellowship, research assistantship or teaching assistantship. Before accepting, obtain a written offer stating the tuition waiver, stipend, health-insurance contribution, number of guaranteed years, summer funding, work duties and renewal rules. Start with each department's “funding” page; for example, Cornell explains how entering doctoral students may receive field fellowships.

Secondary-school and undergraduate exchanges

These programmes are especially relevant because Iraq is directly named in the programme. They are exchanges, not four-year degrees.

Fully funded · Iraq

IYLEP High School

Programme: The Iraqi Young Leaders Exchange Program combines online preparation, a U.S. leadership exchange and a follow-on community action project.

2026 requirements: Age 15–17 on 1 July 2026, at least one completed year and one remaining year of secondary school, graduation in 2027 or later, parent permission and sufficient English for the selected track.

Coverage: Programme and visa costs, transport, housing, meals, insurance and a stipend.

Status: The 2026 cycle is closed. Use these requirements only to prepare; the dates and age calculation in the next official notice will control.

Fully funded · Iraq

IYLEP Undergraduate

Programme: A roughly four-week U.S. leadership programme with online preparation and a six-month community project after participants return to Iraq.

Planning profile: Prior published guidance required enrolment at an Iraqi university, at least one semester remaining after the exchange, age within the cycle's published range, leadership potential and no dual U.S. citizenship.

Coverage: International and local transport, visa and programme costs, housing, meals, insurance and a stipend.

Status: The site was not accepting Iraqi undergraduate applications when checked. Do not rely on an old age range or deadline; use the next official application notice.

Outdated-list warning: Iraq is not on the official TechGirls 2026 participating-country list. Iraqi students should not spend time on that programme unless a future official cycle adds Iraq.

Step-by-step application guide

Begin 12–18 months before your intended enrolment. For fall 2027, many large undergraduate scholarships close between October 2026 and January 2027.

  1. Set your real annual budget. Include tuition, mandatory fees, housing, food, health insurance, books, visa/SEVIS fees, flights and personal costs. Decide the maximum your family can pay without unsafe debt.
  2. Choose the correct applicant type. Select first-year, transfer, graduate or exchange. Previous university enrolment can make you a transfer even if no credits are accepted.
  3. Build a balanced list. Include ambitious full-need universities, competitive full scholarships and more predictable automatic-merit options. Remove any university whose remaining cost is unaffordable.
  4. Check eligibility line by line. Confirm Iraqi citizenship/residency eligibility, age, graduation year, degree, GPA, work experience, subject exclusions and whether the award is for first-year students only.
  5. Plan tests early. Check each admission and scholarship page for English tests and SAT/ACT rules. A test-optional admission policy does not always make the scholarship test-optional; Alabama's automatic awards, for example, use official scores.
  6. Prepare academic records. Obtain official school/university transcripts, graduation certificates and certified English translations. Never alter grades or translate an official record yourself unless the institution explicitly permits it.
  7. Ask for recommendations. Choose teachers, professors or supervisors who know your work. Give them the deadline, programme requirements, CV and examples of your leadership or impact several weeks early.
  8. Write specific essays. Explain your goals, evidence of service/leadership, obstacles you addressed, why the programme fits and how you will use the education in Iraq. Do not copy templates or let AI invent experiences.
  9. Submit every separate form. An admission application does not automatically submit a scholarship, CSS Profile, ISFAA or department-funding application. Use a checklist for each institution.
  10. Apply for aid before admission results. International aid deadlines can be as early as the admission deadline. If the CSS Profile cost is a barrier, ask whether the college offers a fee waiver or accepts an alternative financial statement.
  11. Compare the final offers. Subtract grants and scholarships from the total cost, then add travel and other excluded expenses. Check renewal GPA, maximum semesters and whether funding changes after year one.
  12. Complete the visa process only after admission. Choose a school, provide its required proof of funding, receive Form I-20, pay the current I-901 SEVIS fee, submit DS-160 and apply for an F-1 visa. Follow the current instructions for applicants in Iraq and do not buy final tickets before a visa is issued.

Document checklist

  • Valid Iraqi passport
  • Official transcripts and certificates
  • Certified English translations
  • English test result, if required
  • SAT/ACT result for score-based awards
  • CV or activities list
  • Personal statement and scholarship essays
  • Recommendation letters
  • CSS Profile, ISFAA or university aid form
  • Parent income/tax/employer documents
  • Bank evidence for the uncovered amount
  • Admission and scholarship deadline tracker
Financial-aid forms: Many colleges use the CSS Profile for international applicants. Enter family income, assets and expenses in the home currency unless the form tells you otherwise; the system converts it. Some colleges accept an ISFAA or their own form. Most students in F-1 status are not eligible for U.S. federal student aid, so the key sources are institutional grants, merit scholarships and external fellowships.

How to find more legitimate scholarships

  1. Use the official EducationUSA financial-aid search; filter by degree level and read the nationality rule.
  2. Search the IIE Funding for United States Study database.
  3. On every university site, open “International admissions,” “Financial aid,” “Merit scholarships” and, for graduate study, the department's “Funding” page.
  4. Monitor the official U.S. Embassy in Iraq website for education and exchange announcements, as well as verified AMIDEAST Iraq announcements.
  5. Email the financial-aid office with one precise question: “Is this award open to a citizen and resident of Iraq applying as an international first-year/transfer/graduate student for [term]?” Keep the written answer.
Avoid scholarship scams. Do not pay anyone to “guarantee” admission, a scholarship or a visa. Never send passport scans or bank documents through an unverified social-media account. Confirm the application on a university, U.S. government or official programme domain. A scholarship offer never guarantees an F-1 visa.
Medicine is a special case: Fulbright Iraq excludes clinical medicine and related clinical degrees. U.S. MD programmes are normally graduate-entry, international admission is limited and funding is rare; some schools request proof of funds for all four years. Read the AAMC guidance for international medical-school applicants. Public health and non-clinical research programmes usually have different rules.

After you win: admission, I-20 and visa

According to the U.S. Department of State student-visa guide, degree study requires admission to an SEVP-approved school. The school registers the student in SEVIS and issues Form I-20. The student then pays the I-901 fee, completes DS-160 and applies for the visa. The current F/M visa application fee shown when this guide was checked was $185; check both visa and SEVIS fees again before paying. Be ready to show academic preparation, intent to follow visa rules and how all remaining education and living costs will be paid.

A new-student visa may be issued up to 365 days before the programme starts, but a new student cannot enter the United States more than 30 days before the I-20 start date. Appointment location and processing arrangements can change, so follow the current official instructions for residents of Iraq.
