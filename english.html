1. Where should a complete beginner start?

Your first target: A1 English

English ability is commonly divided into six CEFR levels:

  1. A1: Beginner
  2. A2: Elementary
  3. B1: Intermediate
  4. B2: Upper-intermediate
  5. C1: Advanced
  6. C2: Highly proficient

At the end of A1, a student should be able to:

  • Introduce themselves
  • Say where they live and study
  • Talk simply about family and daily life
  • Ask and answer basic questions
  • Understand slow, clear everyday speech
  • Read short messages and signs
  • Write short sentences and simple messages
Important: A 12-week plan is not a promise of fluency. Progress depends on study time, practice, previous knowledge and consistency.
2. The five rules for learning English
  1. Practise regularly: 30 minutes every day is generally better than one very long lesson each week.
  2. Learn complete phrases: learn “How are you?” rather than memorising only the separate words.
  3. Use the language: listening and reading are not enough. Speak and write too.
  4. Review repeatedly: revisit vocabulary after one day, three days, one week and two weeks.
  5. Accept mistakes: mistakes show what needs more practice.
Do not only learn: “breakfast”
Learn a useful sentence: “I eat breakfast at seven o’clock.”
3. The daily 30–45 minute routine
Time Activity What to do
5–10 minutes Vocabulary Learn five useful words with example sentences.
10 minutes Listening Listen to a short beginner recording two or three times.
5–10 minutes Speaking Repeat the recording and record your own voice.
10 minutes Reading or grammar Read a short text or complete one grammar lesson.
5 minutes Writing Write three to five sentences using today’s vocabulary.

If you only have 15 minutes

  • Listen for five minutes
  • Repeat aloud for five minutes
  • Write three sentences for five minutes
4. Vocabulary: what words should you learn?

Begin with words needed in normal daily situations. Learn approximately five useful words each day.

Order Topic Examples
1 Greetings Hello, goodbye, please, thanks
2 Personal information Name, age, country, city, address
3 Numbers Age, prices, phone numbers
4 Time and dates Days, months, hours, today, tomorrow
5 Family Mother, father, brother, sister
6 School Teacher, student, book, lesson, examination
7 Daily activities Wake up, study, eat, go, sleep
8 Food and drink Water, bread, rice, meat, fruit
9 Home Room, door, table, kitchen, bedroom
10 Town and transport Road, shop, hospital, car, bus
11 Body and health Head, hand, pain, doctor, medicine
12 Weather and clothes Hot, cold, shirt, shoes, coat

How to record a new word

Word: hungry
Meaning: needing food
Example: I am hungry after school.
Related word: food

Do not write a long list of words without examples. Every important word should appear in a useful sentence.

5. Grammar: learn it in this order
Stage Grammar Example
1 Subject pronouns I, you, he, she, it, we, they
2 The verb “to be” I am a student. She is happy.
3 Possessive adjectives My name, your book, his school
4 A, an and the A book, an apple, the teacher
5 Singular and plural nouns One student, two students
6 Present simple I study. He studies.
7 Questions with do and does Do you study? Does she work?
8 There is and there are There is a shop. There are two schools.
9 Can and cannot I can swim. I cannot drive.
10 Present continuous I am studying now.
11 Past simple I visited my friend yesterday.
12 Some, any, much and many Some water. How many books?
13 Comparatives This book is easier than that book.
14 Future plans I am going to study tomorrow.
15 Prepositions In the room, on Monday, at five o’clock
After learning one grammar rule, write five personal examples. Grammar becomes useful when you use it to communicate something real.
6. How to improve listening

Use the three-listen method

  1. First listen: listen without reading. Try to understand the main idea.
  2. Second listen: listen while reading the transcript or subtitles.
  3. Third listen: pause after every sentence and repeat it aloud.

Choose short recordings that:

  • Use beginner vocabulary
  • Have clear audio
  • Include a transcript
  • Are approximately one to five minutes long
Do not begin with fast films without subtitles. If almost every sentence is impossible to understand, choose easier material.
7. How to improve speaking and pronunciation

Speaking practice

  1. Listen to one short sentence.
  2. Pause the recording.
  3. Repeat the sentence aloud.
  4. Copy the speaker’s rhythm and stress.
  5. Record yourself.
  6. Compare your recording with the original.

This technique is sometimes called shadowing.

Important sounds for Arabic-speaking learners

Sounds Examples Practice
P and B park / bark Put paper near your mouth. P creates air.
V and F van / fan Your voice vibrates when saying V.
TH sounds think / this Place the tongue lightly between the teeth.
Final consonants book, hand, helped Do not remove the final sound.
Consonant groups street, school, asked Practise slowly, then increase speed.
The goal is understandable speech—not copying one particular British or American accent.
8. How to improve reading
  1. Choose a short A1 text.
  2. Read once without using a dictionary.
  3. Identify the main topic.
  4. Read again and underline unfamiliar words.
  5. Look up only the most important words.
  6. Write a one-sentence summary.

Good beginner materials

  • Signs and notices
  • Menus
  • Timetables
  • Short messages
  • Simple emails
  • Very short stories
  • Beginner dialogues
Do not translate every word. First try to understand the general meaning from the complete sentence.
9. How to improve writing

Begin with five-sentence paragraphs

My name is Ahmed. I am sixteen years old. I live in Duhok. I am a secondary-school student. I enjoy mathematics and football.

Beginner writing topics

  • Introduce yourself
  • Describe your family
  • Describe your room
  • Explain your daily routine
  • Write about your school
  • Describe what you did yesterday
  • Write your plan for tomorrow
  • Write a short message or email

Writing checklist

  • Does every sentence begin with a capital letter?
  • Does every sentence end with punctuation?
  • Does the sentence contain a subject and verb?
  • Did you check the spelling?
  • Is the verb tense correct?
10. Essential phrases every beginner needs

👋 Meeting people

  • Hello. My name is …
  • What is your name?
  • Nice to meet you.
  • Where are you from?
  • I am from Iraq.

❓ Asking for help

  • Can you help me?
  • I do not understand.
  • Can you repeat that, please?
  • Can you speak more slowly?
  • What does this word mean?

🏫 In class

  • How do you spell it?
  • May I ask a question?
  • Which page is it?
  • Is this answer correct?
  • I have finished.

🛍️ Shopping

  • How much is this?
  • I would like this one.
  • Do you have a smaller size?
  • Can I pay by card?
  • Thank you.

🧭 Directions

  • Where is the hospital?
  • Is it far from here?
  • Go straight.
  • Turn left.
  • Turn right.

🩺 Health

  • I do not feel well.
  • I have a headache.
  • Where is the doctor?
  • I need help.
  • Please call my family.
11. Complete 12-week study plan
Week Main topics Weekly task
1 Alphabet, basic sounds, greetings and names Record a 30-second introduction.
2 Numbers, countries, ages and the verb “to be” Write ten sentences about yourself.
3 Family, possessives, a/an/the and plurals Describe your family in five sentences.
4 Daily routines and present simple Describe one complete school day.
5 Questions, days, dates and time Ask and answer ten simple questions.
6 Home, town, there is/are and prepositions Describe your room or neighbourhood.
7 Food, shopping, some/any and countable nouns Create a shopping dialogue.
8 Present continuous and current activities Describe what five people are doing.
9 Past simple and common irregular verbs Write what you did yesterday.
10 Descriptions and comparative adjectives Compare two cities, people or objects.
11 Future plans, can/cannot and invitations Explain your plan for next weekend.
12 Complete review of listening, speaking, reading and writing Repeat the level test and record a two-minute introduction.

13. Common mistakes to avoid
  • Studying grammar without speaking
  • Learning too many words at once
  • Never reviewing old lessons
  • Translating every individual word
  • Choosing material that is too difficult
  • Being silent because you fear mistakes
  • Using subtitles in your first language forever
  • Studying for hours once and then stopping for days
  • Expecting fluency after several weeks
  • Paying for courses before using quality free resources

15. Your first English lesson today

Step 1: Read these sentences

Hello. My name is Ali.
I am sixteen years old.
I am from Iraq.
I live in Duhok.
I am a student.
I like science.
Nice to meet you.

Step 2: Change the information

Replace the name, age, city and favourite subject with your own information.

Step 3: Speak

Read your introduction aloud three times. Then record it without reading.

Step 4: Write

Write five sentences about yourself without copying.

Step 5: Review tomorrow

Try the same introduction again before beginning the next lesson.

Online safety: Young students should not publish their complete name, school, address, phone number, photographs or private recordings on public websites. Ask a parent or guardian before creating accounts on external services.
