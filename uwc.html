Q1: What is UWC?

United World Colleges (UWC) is a global educational movement. It brings together students from different countries, cultures and backgrounds.

UWC currently has 18 schools and colleges across four continents. Most focus on students aged 16–19, although some also teach younger students.

Q2: What is UWC’s purpose?

UWC’s mission is to make education a force that unites people, nations and cultures for peace and a sustainable future.

Students from different parts of the world live, learn and participate in community activities together. This helps them understand different cultures, cooperate with others and contribute positively to their communities.

Q3: What scholarships does UWC provide?

Students applying through a UWC national committee may receive full or partial financial support. More than 80% of students selected through national committees receive some form of financial support.

The exact amount is not automatically fixed at 50%, 75% or 100%. It depends on:

  • The student’s demonstrated financial need
  • The funding available during that application year
  • The school or college offering the placement
  • What expenses the scholarship is designed to cover

Depending on the award, support may cover some or all of the tuition, accommodation, meals, books, IB examination fees and other necessary expenses.

Q4: What academic programme does UWC offer?

All UWC schools offer the International Baccalaureate Diploma Programme (IBDP) for students aged 16–19.

The IBDP is an academically challenging and internationally respected secondary-school qualification.

The six subject groups

  1. Studies in Language and Literature: usually the student’s strongest language.
  2. Language Acquisition: learning or developing another language.
  3. Individuals and Societies: subjects such as History, Economics, Global Politics or Anthropology.
  4. Sciences: subjects such as Biology, Chemistry, Physics or Computer Science.
  5. Mathematics: mathematical courses chosen according to the student’s ability and goals.
  6. The Arts: subjects such as Visual Arts, Theatre, Music or Dance. Students may sometimes replace this with another subject from Groups 1–4.

Students normally study three subjects at Higher Level and three at Standard Level.

The three core components

  1. Theory of Knowledge (TOK): students examine how knowledge is formed and evaluated.
  2. Creativity, Activity, Service (CAS): students participate in creative work, physical activities and service projects.
  3. Extended Essay (EE): an independent research project resulting in an essay of up to 4,000 words.

Q5: Who can apply through UWC Iraq?

Eligibility changes between application years. According to the currently published UWC Iraq criteria for the upcoming cycle, applicants should:

  • Have a birthdate between 1 September 2009 and 31 December 2010.
  • Be in the fourth or fifth grade of Iraqi secondary education as of 1 September 2026.
  • Not be a sixth-grade student during this application cycle.
  • Be an Iraqi citizen and/or a permanent resident of Iraq.
  • Have the academic ability to complete a demanding international programme.
  • Be willing to improve their English. Fluency is not currently listed as an application requirement.

Students should always check the current official UWC Iraq eligibility page because dates and conditions can change every year.

Q6: What does the application normally include?

The UWC Iraq application process currently lists:

  • An online form containing personal details, academic history, activities and reasons for applying
  • Essays or short written answers
  • References from teachers, mentors or community leaders
  • Academic transcripts and other requested documents
  • Signed permission from a parent or legal guardian

Shortlisted students may later complete written assessments, group activities, interviews and possibly a presentation about a project or initiative.

The publicly available guide does not currently list a passport and national ID as universal initial requirements. Applicants must follow the exact document list displayed in their application form.

What qualities does UWC consider?

UWC does not select students using grades alone. Its published selection criteria include:

  • Intellectual curiosity
  • Commitment to personal and UWC values
  • Respectful teamwork and social competence
  • Resilience, self-awareness and independence
  • Personal responsibility and integrity
  • Ability to manage the academic programme

Community service and personal initiatives can demonstrate some of these qualities, but applicants should describe their genuine experiences rather than completing activities merely to impress the selection committee.

⚠️ Write your application honestly

Your essays and application answers must represent your own experiences, ideas and voice. Do not submit copied or AI-generated answers as though you wrote them yourself.

Always follow the current instructions shown inside the official application. The public UWC Iraq guide does not currently state that every possible use of AI automatically counts as cheating, so this website should not present that unverified statement as an official rule.
